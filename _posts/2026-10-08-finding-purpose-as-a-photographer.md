      My journey as a photographer has gone through different phases. I started
      at a very young age, and back then photography had no deeper meaning for
      me. I wasn’t trying to express myself, make an impact or tell a story. And
      honestly, that was a lot of fun, there was absolutely zero pressure to
      make something specific. It was ultimate freedom. I photographed
      everything from flowers to thunderbolts, and if you think any two photos
      from that period have something to do with each other, you’d be completely
      wrong. There wasn’t a single thought behind any of those images. I’d just
      look at them on my laptop and go: “Yeah, this looks kinda cool.”




      ![](/images/blog/uploads/muzmff5x-746e0mly.jpeg)




      But when I looked back through my archive, I realized those images did
      have something interesting in common. The subjects were all over the
      place, but they showed me what I was really chasing as a photographer:
      light. More specifically, good light. I had no control over it. I never
      used flash or a studio setup, so I was completely dependent on the
      weather. That’s probably also why I have around 100 shoots from golden
      hour.




      ![](/images/blog/uploads/muzmftqf-lfkdrzrx.jpeg)




      After a few years of this, I decided to apply to art school. I was a bit
      naive and overconfident, but I still managed to get through the selection
      process. Together with one of my best friends, I moved to Rotterdam, and
      that’s where my real journey through photography began. I learned about
      analog photography, studio lighting, flash and alternative printing
      techniques. A whole new world opened up in front of me, and at the same
      time my confidence plummeted. Everything I thought I knew turned out to be
      either incomplete or a simplified version of reality. It was humbling, but
      truly necessary.




      ![](/images/blog/uploads/muzmm3zd-9o0x8j71.jpeg)




      Once everything I thought I knew about photography had fallen apart, I was
      ready to soak up all the new information. And that’s exactly what I did.
      Over the following years at the academy, I grew as a maker. I discovered
      where my interests within photography lie and learned how to create work
      that actually makes an impact.


      Looking back, I don’t see those early years as a waste. That kid running
      around with a camera, shooting flowers and thunderstorms without a second
      thought, was already learning to see. He just didn’t know it yet. Art
      school didn’t replace that curiosity, it gave it direction and purpose.
      These days I think a lot more before I take photos, but I’m still chasing
      the same thing I always was: good light. The difference is that now I know
      why.
